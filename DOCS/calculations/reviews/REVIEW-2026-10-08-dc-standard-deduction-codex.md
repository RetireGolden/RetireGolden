## 1. Governing law for DC tax year 2026 — reject

The [Council's B26-0661 legislative record](https://lims.dccouncil.gov/api/Search/GetLegislationDetails/B26-0661) reports Act A26-0418, Law L26-0189, transmission to Congress on August 20, and an effective date of October 2, 2026. I fetched the [enrolled original](https://lims.dccouncil.gov/downloads/LIMS/61627/Meeting3/Enrollment/B26-0661-Enrollment17.pdf?Id=243990) directly and independently extracted its PDF text in memory: §§ 7112, 7113 and 9003 support the deduction, applicability from January 1, 2025, and the congressional-review condition. The [emergency Act 26-416](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/acts/26-416) uses the same relevant amounts and 2030 transition. [H.J.Res. 142 on Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-joint-resolution/142/text) names Act 26-217 alone; it does not name Act 26-418. I found no resolution against Act 26-418 in targeted Congress/GPO searches, consistent with LIMS recording Law 26-189 as effective, but absence of a search result is not conclusive. Congress.gov's live search returned HTTP 403; its indexed H.J.Res. 142 text was accessible. The LIMS page requires JavaScript in the web reader, but its API and enrolled PDF were accessible by direct HTTP.

The historical claim that the amounts remained in force **without a gap** through [Law 26-89](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/laws/26-89) needs qualification. The [DC Attorney General's February 2026 opinion](https://oag.dc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/AG-Opinion-Decoupling-Retroactivity-and-Validity-.pdf) concludes H.J.Res. 142 missed the Home Rule Act deadline and did not repeal that temporary law, while the [White House's February 18 statement](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/02/congressional-bills-h-j-res-142-and-s-3705-signed-into-law/) says the resolution nullified it. The record presents the contested historical continuity as settled and does not cite or disclose that dispute. Qualify the continuity as the District's official legal and administrative position, with the Attorney General opinion and contrary federal statement. This does not unsettle Law 26-189's present application to tax year 2026.

## 2. Figures and worked tax — approve

The enrolled § 7112 and [Act 26-416's readable counterpart](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/acts/26-416) provide the 2026 basic amounts of $15,000 single, $22,500 head of household and $30,000 joint, plus IRC § 63(c)(3)'s additional amount; § 7112 sends years after 2029 back to IRC § 63(c). At constant 2.5% assumed inflation, floor-to-$50 gives 2027 single `floor(15000 × 1.025 / 50) × 50 = 15350`, joint `floor(30000 × 1.025 / 50) × 50 = 30750`, and 2029 single `floor(15000 × 1.025³ / 50) × 50 = 16150`. These are projections, not published DC CPI determinations. The record correctly discloses that the engine's inflation timing differs from the statute on a nonconstant path. The 2030 code changes DC to federal conformity. With the listed DC brackets, $60,000 less $15,000 is $45,000 taxable, yielding `$400 + $1,800 + $325 = $2,525`; $120,000 less $30,000 is $90,000 taxable, yielding `$400 + $1,800 + $1,300 + $2,550 = $6,050`. The [2026 D-40ES](https://otr.cfo.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/otr/publication/attachments/2026_D40ES_Book_wLinks04012026.pdf) indeed prints the older federal estimate amounts.

## 3. Part-year proration — approve

[DC Code § 47-1801.04(44)(D)](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-1801.04) expressly prorates the standard-deduction amounts by **months** of residence. The DC pack selects `standardDeduction: 'months'`, and `prorateParams` multiplies both the basic deduction and age-65 addition by the resident-month fraction. The [2025 D-40 booklet, Calculation C](https://otr.cfo.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/otr/publication/attachments/2025_D40_Book_082026_v1.pdf), instead asks for days lived in DC divided by 365 or 366. The record describes this source difference accurately; it does not silently conflate the two methods.
