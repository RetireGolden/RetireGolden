**Statements, formula, and limits checked.** `buildOptimizerModel` writes the terminal tax-free and taxable buckets at `fmt8(D)`, the traditional and inherited buckets at `fmt8(D × (1 − L))`, and `0.000001` per conversion. `buildOptimizerInput` gets `D` from `planDollarBasis`'s last factor: one-year `1`, two-year `1/1.025`, and 24-year `1/(1.025)^23`, on the same factor the projection publishes. `rawSolutionSolver` wraps the HiGHS writer and discards the highs package's pretty-print parse; `parseRawSolution` reads the raw objective and columns (the documented 16-significant-digit format) and fails closed on unreadable output. The default options are `mip_max_nodes: 5000` and `time_limit: 60`, with caller overrides. `scheduleStatus` maps `Optimal → optimal`, `Solution limit reached → node-limit`, `Time limit reached` or `Iteration limit reached → timeout`, `Infeasible` or `Primal infeasible or unbounded → infeasible`, and any other status by primal feasibility (`feasible` or `infeasible`). A feasible primal publishes the objective, reconstructed tax, and all rows under **any** mapped status; a non-feasible primal publishes null figures, empty rows and conversions, and conversion total 0, also under **any** mapped status. Thus the prose's shorthand that status “says why” applies to HiGHS's explicit statuses; the fallback `infeasible` alone does not prove mathematical infeasibility.
