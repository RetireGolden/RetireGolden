## household-facts — reject

The independent model-dollar calculations reproduce IL `(50,000 − 2,925 × 1/2) × .0495 = 2,402.60625`; MA `(50,000 − 4,400 × 1/2) × .05 = 2,390`; CT `(200 + 25,000 × .045) × 1/2 = 662.50`; WI `[(100,000 − 4,374.40 − 700) bracket tax] × 1/2 = 2,213.7584`; UT `(20,000 × .0445 − 450) × 1/2 = 220`; IA `min((26,000 − 16,100) × .038, 9,200 − 9,000) × 1/2 = 100`; and SC `30,000 × .0199 + 20,000 × .0521 = 1,639`, because the 2026 SCIAD has phased out at $100,000 AGI. These are the cases in `statePartYear.householdFacts.rules.test.ts`. The prior figures in that file follow when the fact-dependent exemptions or credits are omitted. [Illinois Schedule NR](https://tax.illinois.gov/forms/incometax/currentyear/individual/il-1040-schedule-nr-instr.html) prorates the exemption by its income decimal; [Massachusetts' part-year guidance](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/personal-income-tax-exemptions) uses **days**, for which six months is an acknowledged proxy; [Connecticut's CT-1040NR/PY instructions](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/drs/forms/2025/income/2025-ct-1040-nrpy-instructions_1225.pdf) calculate the annual exemption and tax before the source-income ratio. [Wisconsin Form 1NPR](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/TaxForms2025/2025-Form1NPR-Inst.pdf), [Utah TC-40 instructions](https://files.tax.utah.gov/tax/forms/current/tc-40inst.pdf), [Iowa's IA 126 instructions](https://revenue.iowa.gov/taxes/tax-guidance/individual-income-tax/1040-expanded-instructions/credit-nonpart-year-resident), and [South Carolina's 2026 law summary](https://dor.sc.gov/news/information-about-h-4216) support those respective return sequences.

**Oregon is a counterexample.** The test computes annual tax $1,176.375, subtracts the $225 retirement income credit, and then halves both, yielding $475.6875. [Form OR-40-P](https://www.oregon.gov/dor/forms/FormsPubs/form-or-40-p_101-055_2025.pdf) instead multiplies tax by the Oregon percentage on line 45 and subtracts Schedule OR-ASC-NP standard credits on lines 51–53. [Publication OR-17's retirement credit worksheet, code 811](https://www.oregon.gov/dor/forms/FormsPubs/publication-or-17_101-431_2025.pdf) uses the **Oregon-column** pension on lines 1 and 3 and gives no further percentage multiplication for this credit. The resident period's $6,000 pension exceeds the worksheet's $2,500 credit base, so the credit remains $225. Using the pack's model tax, the form ordering is `$1,176.375 × 1/2 − $225 = $363.1875`, $112.50 below the asserted result. The implementation's method-(b) multiplication of `full.taxCredit` by `incomeRatio` is therefore wrong for this credit.

## exclusion-caps — reject

The Decimal worksheet reproduces the tests' stated **code calculations**: NY `$3,183 × (45,000 − 15,000)/70,000 = $1,364.142857…`; DE `$3,224 × (45,000 − 12,500 × 2/3)/67,500 = $1,751.308642…`; WI `$2,029.9364 × (40,000 − 12,000)/80,000 = $710.47774`; WV `$2,500.10 × 1/2 = $1,250.05`; KY `(30,000 − 3,360 − 31,110 × 1/2) × .035 = $387.975`. The DE test's prose says `$1,751.28`, while its assertion's formula is `$1,751.31` to cents. [NY IT-203 instructions](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/2025/inc/it203i_2025.pdf) allow up to $20,000 for the resident taxable period. [Delaware PIT-NON](https://revenuefiles.delaware.gov/2025/PITForms_Instructions/PIT-NON_2025-01_PaperInteractiveIPM.pdf) divides modified Delaware-source income by Delaware AGI; its cited instructions allocate the pension exclusion by the pension-income share. [Wisconsin Schedule M's part-year worksheet](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/TaxForms2025/2025-ScheduleM-Inst.pdf) explicitly multiplies $24,000 by its line 4/line 5 income fraction. [West Virginia Schedule A](https://tax.wv.gov/Documents/PIT/2025/it140.Schedule-A.2025.pdf) uses unreduced West Virginia income over federal AGI. These support the worked policies, subject to each form's rounding, which the worksheet discloses as unmodeled.

**Kentucky's cap is established, contrary to the derivation's access caveat.** The official [2025 Schedule P instructions](https://revenue.ky.gov/Forms/Schedule%20P%20%282025%29.pdf) tell Form 740-NP filers to report pension income received while a Kentucky resident, then Part III line 3 takes the lesser of that pension income and **$31,110**; the [2025 Form 740-NP](https://revenue.ky.gov/Forms/25_42A740-NP.pdf) carries the exclusion in its Kentucky column. The [Schedule A instructions](https://revenue.ky.gov/Forms/740-NP%20Schedule%20A%20%282025%29.pdf) separately confirm the standard deduction is not prorated. For the test's $20,000 resident pension, the whole cap excludes $20,000; modeled 2026 tax is `(30,000 − 3,360 − 20,000) × .035 = $232.40`, not $387.975. Kentucky should not be grouped with unresolved caps. The fallback's incomplete status is cautious for genuinely unresolved states, but `computeSplitYearResult` emits it whenever there is *any* positive retirement income and an age-qualified capped rule, including a $1 pension far below the cap; it does not test whether the cap binds. The prose describing incomplete only when a cap binds does not match that condition.

## allocator — approve

`residencySpans` places January–June and July–December in disjoint spans. `spanShare` gives a current-year dated row shares 1 and 0, or 0 and 1; undated rows get 1/2 each. Independently, for the allocator test's $140,000 ordinary income including a $40,000 March conversion, plus $6,000 gains and $2,000 qualified dividends, the two federal-AGI-item allocations are `$94,000` and `$54,000`, summing to the annual `$148,000`. Its retirement row's $40,000 included/gross/conversion amounts all follow March; an undated $40,000 row splits to $20,000 and $20,000 with the listed numeric fields scaled together. A November $10,000 direct QCD goes wholly to the later slice, while an undated one halves each numeric field. The `paidMonths: 6` Social Security case assigns a July–December claimant 0/100% and a full-year claimant 50/50%, giving the documented 1/3 and 2/3 weighted shares, which sum to one. The dollar amount spread by months is exposed as `partYear.undatedIncomeSpreadByMonths`. Pension, annuity and wage annual amounts are treated as twelve-month streams because the current projection pays those sources for whole years; this approximation is disclosed. MA/VA/UT pension, NJ IRA and year-end-state HSA basis results are assembled after slice pricing, once in the annual result. Within these modeled inputs I found no duplicated or missing row across the two slices.

## u1-annuity — reject

`preStartEvents.figures.test.ts` defines U1 as the example couple, a November 2026 move from KY to FL, a 2026 Roth fill, and an optional $100,000 annuity purchase. The example has KY residence and $225,000 annual wages; the annuity purchase alters funding and thus the conversion. The test's prose reports retirement exclusion amounts `$5,277` with the annuity and `$31,156` without it. The official [Kentucky Schedule P](https://revenue.ky.gov/Forms/Schedule%20P%20%282025%29.pdf) includes IRA income reported on federal Form 1040 line 4 and limits its 740-NP column to income received while resident, so allowing qualifying conversion rows to meet the pension exclusion is directionally justified. A larger exclusion in the no-annuity case reduces that case's tax and makes the annuity effect more negative. Yet the implementation uses the **ten-month share of the cap** where Schedule P uses the whole $31,110 per taxpayer against resident receipts, and it spreads the undated conversion through ten months. Thus the claimed `−$151,684.19` is not established as Kentucky-form-correct. I can reproduce the exclusion mechanism and the separate six-month Kentucky counterexample above, but cannot independently derive the $4,068.38 lifetime change or ending net worth: the snapshot gives no accepted U1 year-by-year conversion, tax and balance ledger, and running the engine is prohibited.

## relocation — reject

`relocationScenarioPatch` creates an actual July move when a candidate has `moveYear` and otherwise replaces the state from the start. `compareRelocationCandidates` runs the resulting plan's full projection and records yearly state tax; a candidate's tax delta is candidate minus baseline. The example couple starts in KY with wages, accounts and a Roth strategy, so a move-year candidate can change its Kentucky slice; glidepath-allocation and static-allocation-control share the same CA single-filer household, balances, income, Roth and QCD settings, while their asset return/allocation policies differ. A reduction in candidate state tax would make the reported candidate deltas negative, so the sign is plausible. The four reported magnitudes (`−549.35`, `−283.09`, `−1,919.68`, and `−1,198.18` twice) cannot be independently checked from the snapshot: no candidate state, move year/month, metric definition or pre/post yearly tax lines accompany them. The Kentucky cap conflict above could also affect any KY move-year candidate with qualifying retirement income. I therefore cannot approve the asserted amounts or their source-derived directions on this record; this is an evidence limit, not a claim that every candidate result is wrong.
