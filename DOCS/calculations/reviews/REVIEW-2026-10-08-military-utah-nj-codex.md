## 1. WI, IN, MN, LA, ME, MI, OK, PA — **approve**

The characterized military retired pay is removed in full before general pension rules. With $100,000 as the only income, the state taxable base is zero in each state, hence **$0 tax**. The conditions differ: [Wisconsin Schedule SB](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/TaxForms2025/2025-ScheduleSB-Inst.pdf) includes SBP and keeps the amount out of its later retirement subtraction; [Indiana IC 6-3-2-4](https://iga.in.gov/ic/2026/Title_6/Article_3/Chapter_2.pdf) reaches the retiree or surviving spouse at any age; [Minnesota 290.0132 subd. 21](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/290.0132) includes Title 10 SBP but excludes claimants taking its separate past-service credit; and [Louisiana 47:44.2](https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=102134) exempts federal retirement apart from its age-65 allowance. [Maine 5122(2)(M-2)](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec5122.html) takes military benefits outside the ordinary cap and defines a primary recipient to include a surviving spouse. [Michigan 206.30](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/mileg.aspx?objectName=mcl-206-30&page=getObject) reduces the remaining general pension maximum by the military amount. [Oklahoma Form 511](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/tax/documents/forms/individuals/current/511-Pkt.pdf) treats military retirement separately from the $10,000 ordinary retirement limb. [Pennsylvania 61 Pa. Code 101.6(c)(3)](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/061/chapter101/s101.6.html) excludes uniformed-service retired pay at any age. The code limits SBP to the full exemption where supported (WI, IN, MN, and a Maine surviving spouse); MI, OK and PA keep their general treatment. Louisiana's cited rule addresses federal retirement payments rather than creating a separate SBP promise.

## 2. Colorado — **approve**

[Colorado's tax guidance](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/agencies/legislative-council-staff/individual-income-tax%C2%A0) distinguishes the under-55 military $15,000 subtraction through 2028 from the ordinary pension subtraction at 55 ($20,000) and 65 ($24,000). The last uses the 2026 federal age addition and senior deduction already in the taxable-income base. The enacted-2029 pack removes only the under-55 limb.

## 3. Maryland — **approve**

[Tax-General 10-207(q)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtg&enactments=false&section=10-207) includes death benefits and grants $12,500 under 55 or $20,000 from 55. On $100,000, the independent taxable bases are $84,100, $76,600 and $36,000; the Maryland brackets yield **$3,942.25, $3,586.00 and $1,657.50** at ages 45, 60 and 65.

## 4. Georgia — **approve**

The [IT-511 instructions](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download) support $17,500 for a recipient under 62 and another $17,500 when that person's Georgia earned income exceeds $17,500, plus a separate full subtraction for a deceased veteran's surviving family member. At 60, $100,000 retired pay gives `(100,000 − 15,000 − 17,500) × 4.99% = $3,368.25`; adding $20,000 wages gives `(120,000 − 15,000 − 35,000) × 4.99% = $3,493.00`. SBP as a survivor benefit gives zero on the isolated $100,000 case. [Signed HB 266](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/hb-266/download) and [Georgia DOR's enacted-law summary](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-legislative-summary/download) confirm **tax years from 2027**, **under age 65**, **up to $65,000 per qualifying spouse**, **no earnings test**, and **no combination with the general retirement exclusion**. The 2027 pack has those age and cap terms. Wage streams are the model's narrower proxy for Georgia earned income, as disclosed.

## 6. Montana — **approve**

[MCA 15-30-2120(3)(n), (8), (9)](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0150/chapter_0300/part_0210/section_0200/0150-0300-0210-0200.html) and [Montana DOR](https://revenue.mt.gov/taxes/individual-income-tax/working-military-retirement-exemption) support the lesser of 50% of retired pay or Montana earned income, and 50% of qualifying SBP without that earned-income limit. ## 7. Kansas — **approve**

[K.S.A. 79-32,117(c)(vii)](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch79/079_032_0117.html) names retirement benefits earned for service in the armed forces, so a characterized military-retirement row needs no separate named-plan code. ## 8. South Carolina and Delaware — **approve**

[South Carolina 12-6-1171](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c006.php) excludes qualifying military retired pay and survivor benefits that are not subject to a premature-distribution penalty; [Delaware's pension instructions](https://revenuefiles.delaware.gov/2025/PITForms_Instructions/Instructions/PIT-RES_Instructions_2025-01.pdf) exclude Box 7 code 1/penalized early distributions from its pension relief. A Title 10 military pension or SBP annuity is not an IRC 72(t) qualified-plan early withdrawal, so marking it nonpremature at 45 is supported. South Carolina then deducts all $100,000 (**$0 tax**); Delaware's under-60 military limb deducts **$12,500**, leaving the cited **$4,544.00** tax. The projection permits an explicit contrary early-distribution fact to override the inference.

## 9. District of Columbia — **approve**

[D.C. Code 47-1803.02(a)(2)(N)(ii)](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-1803.02) excludes federal-government survivor benefits received by someone at least 62 at year end. An SBP annuity meets that description; the ordinary federal/military pension $3,000 limb in (N)(i) expired before 2015. The $100,000 isolated SBP example is fully excluded at 62 (**$0 tax**), and not at 61.

## 10. Missouri — **approve**

[RSMo 143.121.3(12)](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=143.121) concerns retirement from the taxpayer's own armed-forces service. An SBP recipient takes the [143.124.5 public-pension subtraction](https://www.revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=143.124), capped by the maximum Social Security benefit and reduced by any Social Security exemption. [Missouri DOR's pension FAQ](https://dor.mo.gov/faq/taxation/individual/pension.html) explicitly confirms both the SBP classification and **$48,967 for tax year 2026**. The [February 2026 legislative fiscal note](https://senate.mo.gov/FiscalNotes/2026-1/5639S.03P.ORG.pdf) instead says $49,824 while estimating a *proposed* full SBP exclusion; it is not filing guidance, and the origin of that discrepancy is not explained there. For a $100,000 SBP annuity with zero Social Security offset, the operative DOR figure leaves `100,000 − 16,100 − 48,967 = 34,933` taxable and **$1,461.22** tax. Using the fiscal-note number would give **$1,420.94**. The live tax-year table is the stronger authority for the current cap.
