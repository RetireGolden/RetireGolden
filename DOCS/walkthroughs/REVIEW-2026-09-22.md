## 2. Disagreements

**Row 82 only.** It is a mechanism statement, not a published figure, and no numeric figure changes.

*Where we part.* The deriver's premise is right: a cash draw adds no income, so tax does not depend on the draw
size. But the funding root is not seeded with the post-tax need. `annualFundingFixedPoint` calls
`solveFundingRoot(spendingNeedBeforeTax, …)`, where
`spendingNeedBeforeTax = max(0, input.spendingUsesBeforeTax − input.baseCashInflows)`.

*Contract text that decides it.* The `annualFundingFixedPoint.ts` header: "WHAT IT TAKES: the pre-tax cash need, the
cash/healthcare facts needed to size a coordinated HECM draw, …, and one evaluator for a candidate withdrawal need."
The input doc on `spendingUsesBeforeTax`: "Expenses plus contributions, before tax and penalties." Convergence is
`Math.abs(evaluation.requiredNeed - need) <= tolerance` (0.005), with `need = evaluation.requiredNeed` on each direct
iteration.

*The two evaluations.*
- Evaluation 1 runs at need = 116,634.80 − 105,499.320754716981 = 11,135.479245283019. It returns
  `requiredNeed = max(0, 116,634.80 + 0 + 11,238.37536 + 0 − 105,499.320754716981) = 22,373.854605283019`. The
  residual is 11,238.38 > 0.005, so it has not converged.
- Evaluation 2 runs at need = 22,373.854605283019. The draw is still all cash and the tax is unchanged, so
  requiredNeed reproduces itself. It converges, and this evaluation is the one accepted.

The evaluation count is 2 (no HECM capacity, ACA inactive). The count is published only through
`annualAcaResultPublication` in ACA-active years, so no field on this plan's row can observe it. A test must not
assert it.

*Suggested wording:* "converges on the second evaluation. The first runs at the pre-tax need 11,135.48 and returns
the post-tax need 22,373.85, which then reproduces itself."

**C1. Row 11, "The pack's `socialSecurity.colaPct` 2.8 is … used only by the fixed mode and even then compounds from

`year2026.socialSecurity.colaPct = 2.8` as the stated fixed `ssCola` rate". `rules/calculations/socialSecurity.ts:239`

unaffected.

**C2. Rounding paragraph, "the worksheets for these families all set the fixture tolerance at an absolute $0.005."**
Several cited worksheets set a different tolerance:
- `rmd-uniform-lifetime-divisor`: "tolerance: exact because the selected balance is an exact multiple of the
  published divisor".
- `federal-taxable-social-security-tiers`: "exact because all arithmetic resolves to whole dollars".
- `federal-ordinary-bracket-tax`: "exact, because every input and intermediate product is a whole dollar".
- `federal-amt-screen`, `federal-standard-deduction-age-65` and `qcd-income-offset-qualified-slice`: "exact".
- `medicare-magi-composition`: "exact to the cent when inputs are exact currency values".
- `social-security-cola-factor` and `delayed-retirement-credit-factor`: "`1e-12`".

$0.005 is what the dollar compositions state: `social-security-benefit-annual`, `portfolio-need-annual`,
`medicare-base-part-b-premium`, `spending-healthcare-annual`, `spending-total-annual`, `withdrawals-total-annual`
and `accounts-investable-total-annual`. It equals `ANNUAL_FUNDING_TOLERANCE_PLAN_DOLLARS = 0.005`
(`projection/moneyTolerance.ts:11`). The recommendation stands: compare exact values within 0.005, because this
plan's figures are not whole dollars. The claim that all worksheets say so does not.

**C3. Section 2 intro, "`DOCS/architecture.md` ("Simulation core") gives the same order."** `architecture.md`
l.111–114 reads: "income → contributions (limit-enforced) → spending need (phased + healthcare incl.
IRMAA(MAGI[y−2]) / ACA) → RMDs → withdrawals + Roth conversions per strategy → **taxes via fixed-point iteration** …
→ growth → end-of-year balances". The `simulate.ts` header reads: "ages → income → expenses (incl. debt service) →
capped contributions → fixed-point tax/withdrawal iteration → apply flows → property events → growth → snapshot".
The two lists differ step by step: contributions and spending swap places, and RMDs are a separate step only in
`architecture.md`. They agree on the only point the walkthrough needs: growth follows the year's flows.

## 3. Citations I could not confirm as stated (exact text found)

## 4. Ambiguity items, my additions, and the central claim

### The central claim: what each contract documents and what it leaves unstated

- **The aggregate conversion phase** (`annualAggregateRothConversionPhase.ts`). Its comments document that a
  conversion is a rollover inside one individual's own accounts, and that the shared policy module decides who
  converts how much (623–636). They define the published desired amount as "The household amount that policy was
  asked for, before it trimmed an owner who has nowhere to convert to" (457–464). The phase also carries the two trim
  warnings (680–690) and the per-owner debit and credit loops (704–845), and its shortfall warning leaves trims out
  (846–880). Unstated: that the sized amount is neither re-solved nor reassigned after a trim, and what that does to
  a fill-to-target metric.
- **The owner allocation helper** (`actions/aggregateRothConversionOwnerAllocation.ts`). Its header states the whole
  policy: slice by owner, drain only that owner's accounts, credit only a Roth that owner holds, weight by gross
  balance, split by exact-cent largest remainder, skip the split when one owner converts, and leave the trimmed owner
  out of the shortfall total. The `convertibleTargetPlanDollars` doc explains that exclusion. Unstated: why a trimmed
  slice is dropped rather than moved to an owner who could lawfully convert it. The helper knows nothing of targets
  by design, so it cannot say.
- **Registry record `irc-408-d-3-A-i-conversion-benefits-the-distributee`** (`iraBasisAndRollovers.ts` 400,
  `settled`). It states the statute and the projection's behavior in so many words: it "splits the sized household
  amount between owners pro rata by exact-cent largest remainder, drains only that owner’s own convertible accounts,
  credits only a Roth account that owner owns, and trims the slice of an owner who holds no Roth of their own --
  naming that person in a warning". Its `conventionRationale` makes the split rule an engine convention. Unstated:
  the choice not to reassign or re-size, and the consequence that a bracket or tier target is missed.
- **The `YearResult.rothConversion` comment** (`types/result.ts` 244–255). It states the sizing semantics and one
  reduction, the RMD reserve. Unstated: the owner split and the trim. This household contradicts its sizing
  sentence (N1).
- **The `YearResult.aggregateRothConversionAllocationDesired` comment** (result.ts 322–349). This is the one field
  contract that names the case: `rothConversion` "for a household with an owner who holds no Roth IRA is strictly
  less than the figure the policy was handed: that owner's slice is dropped, and the difference never converts."
- **`roth-conversion-annual`.** The worksheet has a single filer, no benefits, one pooled balance and a $0.005
  tolerance. The calculation record (`rules/calculations/roth.ts` 47) says the row "publishes the gross movement
  actually executed, capped by the traditional balance left after the RMD reserve", and its limits name the balance
  and the RMD reserve as the only reductions. Both say nothing about owners, the split or the trim.
- **One more documented source the deriver did not cite.** `DOCS/features/optimizer.md` 240–243 says "an owner with
  no Roth IRA is trimmed, exactly as the ledger trims them, and the household converts less than the winner's figure
  as a result." By contrast, `DOCS/features/roth-and-withdrawals.md` 20–21 and 44 and `DOCS/features/README.md` 277–278
  and 288 describe fill-to-target as converting "up to a chosen ceiling", and the first says "a schedule is a year and

**Status of the central claim: documented rule.** The split, the own-Roth destination, the trim and the warning are
