## 4. Social Security program parameters (2026)

- **COLA 2026:** 2.8%.
- **A PIA from earnings, in the first year's dollars:** the bend-point formula gives the PIA of the eligibility year (the year the worker turns 62). 42 U.S.C. 415(i)(2)(A)(ii)-(iii) raises it by the cost-of-living increase of that year and every later one, flooring to the dime each time, whenever the person claims. The engine applies SSA's published increases (1975-2025) from the eligibility year through the year before the projection's first year, and the plan's COLA assumption, with a warning, for a year SSA has not announced (`usc-42-415-i-2-A-pia-cost-of-living-since-eligibility`). Until 2026-09-27 it used the eligibility-year PIA as if it were current: a worker born in 1960 with a 2022 PIA of $2,846.40 was paid that, not $3,364.40, in 2027. An entered PIA is taken to be current already.
- **Taxable wage base:** $184,500.
- **Earnings test:** $24,480/yr below FRA ($1 withheld per $2 over); $65,160 in the year FRA is reached ($1 per $3); none from FRA. Withheld months are **credited back at FRA** — the relevant retirement, spousal, or survivor factor is recomputed as if claimed that many months later (SSA's "adjustment of the reduction factor," ARF), which reduces the early-claim reduction up to FRA. The engine models both sides (withholding + ARF) on an **annual approximation**: whole withheld months per calendar year rather than monthly accounting, so the first claim year is prorated by its payable months (`poms-rs-00615-482-arf-crediting-months`, `usc-42-403-f-1-earnings-test-month-charging`).
- **Grace-year monthly earnings test:** in a first grace year, 20 CFR 404.435 (with POMS RS 02501.030) can pay a full benefit for a non-service month even when annual earnings are substantial. The Plan carries only annual wages, so the engine applies the annual earnings test only — a registered approximation (`cfr-20-404-435-grace-year-monthly-earnings-test`).
- Claiming 62–70 with **monthly** granularity; early reduction 5/9%/mo (first 36) + 5/12%/mo; delayed credits 2/3%/mo to 70 for births after 1/1/1943, and the lower 20 CFR 404.313(b)(2) rate for earlier births (already in v1 engine).
- **Deemed filing and the dual-entitlement price:** when a person is eligible for both old-age and spouse benefits, [42 U.S.C. §402(r)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title42-section402&num=0&edition=prelim) treats the applications as filed together (for people who attain 62 after 2015), and the Plan represents the pair with one `claimAge` (`usc-42-402-r-1-2-deemed-filing-old-age-and-spousal`). The spouse benefit starts in the later of that claim and the month the worker's benefit starts (for a divorced spouse, the first month the ex is 62 throughout, POMS RS 00202.005 B.2.a). The ledger pays the own benefit plus the spouse reduction factor at that month applied to `max(0, 0.5 × workerPIA - ownPIA)` (42 U.S.C. 402(q)(3)(B), (k)(3)(A)), and, when the own benefit carries delayed credits, the larger of that benefit and the combined amount without them (POMS RS 00615.694), for a current and a divorced spouse alike (`usc-42-402-q-3-B-k-3-A-current-spouse-dual-entitlement`). A two-person household and configured claim dates do not prove SSA eligibility, application, or entitlement month; the Plan does not offer a restricted spouse-only claim, which only people born before January 2, 1954 could still make. Until 2026-09-27 only simultaneous early claims were priced this way; other couples and every divorced spouse got the larger of the own benefit and the reduced half.
- **Family maximum:** retirement/survivor family maximum is PIA-based: 150% of the first family-max bend tier, 272% of the next tier, 134% of the next tier, and 175% above the third bend point, rounded down to the next dime. RetireGolden applies this to the current-spouse auxiliary on a worker record (worker benefit unaffected; auxiliary capped to remaining room), including when the worker is on SSDI — a registered approximation of the AIME-based disability family maximum (`usc-42-403-a-6-ssdi-family-maximum`). Child/dependent auxiliaries remain out of scope (`usc-42-402-d-2-ssdi-child-auxiliary`, `usc-42-402-d-2-child-survivor-benefit`).
- **WEP/GPO:** the engine has no WEP or GPO adjustment. That absence is correct for the forward projections from the current pack year because the Social Security Fairness Act repealed GPO and WEP for title II monthly benefits payable after December 2023; the pre-2024 rule is outside the Plan and projection horizon (`pl-118-273-sec-2-3-wep-gpo-repeal`).
- **AIME initial-computation window:** for an ordinary initial old-age computation with no disability period or prior disability entitlement, computation base years are calendar years after 1950 and before the year of first old-age entitlement; elapsed years run from 1951 or age 22 through the year before 62. The engine uses only that elapsed span for initial AIME, so age-21 and age-62-through-pre-entitlement earnings do not enter (`usc-42-415-b-2-b-ii-iii-initial-computation-base-window`). This is separate from post-entitlement recomputation (`usc-42-415-f-2-post-entitlement-pia-recomputation`).
- **AIME annual indexing:** for the same ordinary initial old-age path, 20 CFR 404.211(d)(3) rounds each indexed year to the nearer penny before the monthly average is floored to a dollar. The engine floors each indexed year to a whole dollar (`cfr-20-404-211-d-3-indexed-earnings-nearer-penny`). A missing future AWI or bend-point year uses the latest published table as a stand-in (`usesStandInForFutureTables`) rather than refusing the computation.
- **AIME computation-year count:** for the same ordinary initial old-age path, old-age computation years are elapsed years minus 5, with elapsed years beginning at 1951 (or age 22 if later). The engine's window starts at 1951 or age 22, whichever is later, and drops the five lowest years, so a 1951-floor elapsed count of 39 is averaged over 34 years (`usc-42-415-b-2-a-i-computation-years-five-year-dropout`; until 2026-09-27 it used 35).
- **Earnings above the base:** 42 U.S.C. 415(e)(1) does not count a year's earnings above that year's contribution and benefit base ($3,600 for 1951-54 up to the section 430 base from 1975). The engine carries SSA's bases for every year from 1937 and caps each year before indexing it; until 2026-09-27 the table began in 1979 and earlier years were capped at the latest base (`usc-42-415-e-1-earnings-above-the-base-not-counted`).
- **Post-entitlement PIA recomputation:** covered earnings in a year for any part of which the worker is entitled to old-age benefits can require a higher PIA under 42 USC 415(f)(2) and 20 CFR 404.211, effective the following January when the increase is at least one dollar. The engine resolves earnings-derived PIA once before the projection loop and does not feed later wages back — a registered approximation (`usc-42-415-f-2-post-entitlement-pia-recomputation`).
- **Trust fund:** combined OASDI depletion projected ~2034 with ~83% of scheduled benefits payable (17% cut) per the **2026 Trustees Report**. Offer a scenario toggle (default off; user-adjustable year/%).
- **Survivor (widow(er)) benefit precision:**
  - **Survivor FRA** follows the section 416(l) age-60-attainment schedule, separate from and earlier than retirement FRA: 65y0m for born ≤1939, **65y2m–65y10m for 1940–44**, **66y0m for 1945–56**, 66y2m–66y10m for 1957–61, and **67 for 1962+** (20 CFR 404.409(b) and POMS RS 00615.301 print the same rows). The engine derives it from the retirement schedule two birth years earlier, since a survivor born in a given year turns 60 in the same calendar year a worker born two years earlier turns 62 (`usc-42-416-l-survivor-fra-age-60-attainment-cohorts`).
  - **Early-claim widow(er) reduction:** claiming survivor between **60** and survivor FRA reduces the benefit by up to **28.5%** at age 60 (linear monthly proration), so the floor payable is **71.5%** of the survivor base. (A *disabled* widow(er) can claim at 50 — out of scope for a retirement planner.) The reduction runs from the first month of widow(er) entitlement, not from an earlier own claim (42 U.S.C. 402(q)(6)(A)(iii), (q)(3)(E)): the engine uses the later of the survivor's own claim and January after the year of death, the first month the ledger pays the survivor (the law would also allow the month of death, 20 CFR 404.621(a)(4)(ii), but the ledger pays nothing for it), and at the survivor FRA credits back only months in which the widow(er) benefit itself was withheld (402(q)(7); `usc-42-402-q-6-A-iii-widow-reduction-from-entitlement-month`). Until 2026-09-27 it reduced the widow(er) benefit at the survivor's own claim age.
  - **Survivor base = the deceased's PIA, or the deceased's actual benefit when that is larger.** A surviving spouse at/above survivor FRA receives the deceased's PIA, including the deceased's delayed-retirement credits when the deceased delayed past FRA (42 U.S.C. 402(e)(2)(A) and (C)); an early claim by the deceased lowers the survivor only through the limit below.
  - **Worker who dies before claiming:** the widow(er) can be entitled from as early as the month of death (at 60 or older, on application, which reaches back at most 6 months), and the base is the PIA plus only the delayed-retirement credits earned before the month of death (none if the death came before FRA), with no early-claim reduction and no RIB-LIM, because the deceased was never entitled to a reduced benefit. The engine does the same, whatever claim age the plan configured for the deceased: the ledger is annual, so it treats December of the last year alive, the latest month the plan's life age allows, as the death month (credits through November; a real death earlier in that year would let the survivor be paid up to eleven months sooner) and starts the survivor benefit the next January, or at the survivor's own entered claim age if that is later (`usc-42-402-e-survivor-of-worker-who-died-before-claiming`). Each credit is worth the 20 CFR 404.313(b)(2) rate for the deceased's date of birth. A death before 62 brings in the death PIA and the 402(e)(2)(B) indexed PIA; the engine uses the PIA as entered, stated as a limit on the survivor-benefit calculation.
  - **RIB-LIM ("widow's limit"):** when the deceased was ever entitled to an old-age benefit reduced for an early claim, 42 U.S.C. 402(e)(2)(D) and POMS RS 00615.320 test the limit on the widow(er) benefit *after* reduction for age: if that after-reduction amount exceeds both the deceased's actual reduced benefit and 82.5% of PIA, the payable amount is the larger of those two limits. The engine does the same: `min(max(PIA, actual) × survivor factor, max(actual, 82.5% × PIA))` for a deceased who claimed early, and no limit otherwise (`poms-rs-00615-320-rib-lim-after-survivor-reduction`). Until 2026-09-27 the engine took the limit first and reduced it for age, which paid an early survivor of an early claimant too little (a $2,400 PIA claimed at 62, survivor at 62: $1,576.93 a month rather than $1,911.43).
  - **Documented simplifications:** living divorced-spouse eligibility applies a 10-year marriage gate, a currently-single gate, and an age-62 gate on the ex from the calendar year of the first month the ex is 62 throughout (the year the ex turns 62, or the next year for an ex born in December after the 2nd), and does not carry worker entitlement, fully-insured status, or years since divorce — so an SSDI-entitled ex under 62 is refused and a not-yet-entitled age-62 ex divorced one year is admitted (`cfr-20-404-331-living-divorced-spouse-eligibility`). Ordinary-widow eligibility uses the 9-month duration, age-60, and remarriage gates on `relationship: deceased`, including after a remarriage at or after 60 while still married; those gates are subject to the other 404.335 entitlement requirements (application may already be met under 404.335(b)(1)–(4) without a new application; 404.335(d) own-old-age-benefit restriction is a pricing comparison the candidate menu does not adjudicate). Historical remarriage before 60 is treated as an unconditional forfeiture even when the claimant is now single, and a coupled household with `remarriedAtAge: null` still receives a candidate without establishing a 404.335(e) exception (`cfr-20-404-335-ordinary-widow-eligibility`). Surviving-divorced eligibility uses the separate `relationship: surviving-divorced` path with the settled 10-year marriage-immediately-before-divorce duration in 404.336(a)(2) (`cfr-20-404-336-surviving-divorced-spouse-eligibility`). The same age-60 gate and a historical `remarriedAtAge` gate modeled like the ordinary survivor path apply on the non-disabled path; current marital status and 404.336(e)(2)–(3) disabled-remarriage exceptions are not adjudicated (`cfr-20-404-336-e-surviving-divorced-remarriage`). Valid-marriage, application, own-benefit, and disability facts remain outside both records. Legacy saved plans that still use `relationship: deceased` keep the ordinary-widow nine-month path. The 9-month statutory duration exceptions, the alternative 404.335(a)(3)/(a)(4) relationship qualifications, and the divorced-spouse remarriage-continuation exception are outside the Plan (`cfr-20-404-335-a-widow-duration-exceptions`, `cfr-20-404-332-b-3-divorced-spouse-remarriage-continuation`). Separate survivor-vs-own claim ages for a current spouse are not modeled (the step-up is paid from the survivor's own claim age and reduced from the later of that claim and the month of death). Survivors are exempt from deemed filing and may use the legally available survivor↔own switch, but the Plan cannot express its two claim ages (`usc-42-402-r-survivor-deemed-filing-exemption`). The disabled-widow(er) age-50 entry is out of scope.
- **Social Security disability (SSDI):**
  - **Benefit = the worker's PIA with *no* early-retirement reduction** — the defining difference from early *retirement* claiming. A disabled worker receives their full PIA regardless of age at onset (which can be well before 62). Modeling input is a **disability-onset age**, not a medical adjudication.
  - **Eligibility (insured status):** generally **20 quarters of coverage in the last 40** (the "20/40" recent-work test) before onset, with the test relaxed for younger workers; plus the SSA disability definition (inability to engage in substantial gainful activity due to a medically determinable impairment expected to last ≥12 months or end in death). Modeled as an input assumption (the planner does not adjudicate disability).
  - **Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA):** earnings above the monthly SGA limit generally stop SSDI. **2026 SGA is $1,690/mo** ($2,830/mo if statutorily blind) — SSA, Cost-of-Living Increase and Other Determinations for 2026, 90 FR 49047; the $1,620 and $2,700 figures are the 2025 amounts it supersedes. The engine applies an annual approximation: wages over SGA × 12 suspend SSDI for that year. The trial-work period and extended period of eligibility are registered approximations (`cfr-20-404-1592-trial-work-period`, `cfr-20-404-1592a-extended-period-of-eligibility`); expedited reinstatement and blind SGA are out of scope (`cfr-20-404-1592b-expedited-reinstatement`, `cfr-20-404-1584-blind-sga-monthly-amount`).
  - **Auto-conversion at FRA:** SSDI **converts to the retirement benefit at FRA at the same dollar amount** (the PIA — continuous, no jump). This is why SSDI matters to a *retirement* planner: it bridges from onset to FRA, then becomes the ongoing retirement benefit. The SSDI recipient earns **no delayed-retirement credits** (the benefit is already being paid), so the PIA persists from onset through life (`usc-42-423-a-2-cfr-20-404-317-ssdi-full-pia-fra-conversion`). The five-month waiting period is a registered approximation (`usc-42-423-c-2-ssdi-five-month-waiting-period`); a reduced retirement claim that precedes later disability onset is also approximated (`usc-42-423-a-2-402-q-retirement-claim-before-disability-onset`).
  - **Disability AIME / freeze:** for an established period of disability, computation-base years wholly within that period and elapsed years wholly or partly within it are excluded unless counting the disability years gives a higher PIA. A pre-62 disability computation also uses the applicable disability benchmark for its indexing year and, for post-June-1980 DIB, the one-for-five computation-year dropout subject to a two-computation-year minimum; for benefits payable after June 1981, a separate child-care dropout may apply when its requirements are met. The Plan carries only an annual `disability.onsetAge` assumption; it does not establish the period, insured status, entitlement, prior-DIB, child-care-dropout, or higher-alternate-computation facts. The earnings→AIME helper ignores onset and continues to use age-62 retirement indexing, the ordinary age-22-through-61 window, and ordinary five-year/top-35 selection, so its earnings-derived SSDI PIA remains a registered approximation (`usc-42-415-b-2-b-disability-freeze-aime-exclusion`).
  - **Taxation & Medicare:** SSDI is taxed under the same provisional-income tiers as retirement benefits (the engine includes it in `incomes.socialSecurity`, so the AGI→SS-tax→IRMAA/ACA cascade works unchanged). SSDI grants Medicare after a 24-month wait — **note-only** for now.
  - **Scope:** the worker's own SSDI + FRA conversion is modeled. The current-spouse auxiliary on an SSDI worker and the family maximum applied to that path are produced by the generic retirement/survivor machinery with registered approximations (`usc-42-402-c-2-ssdi-spouse-auxiliary`, `usc-42-403-a-6-ssdi-family-maximum`). Living-child SSDI auxiliaries remain out of scope (`usc-42-402-d-2-ssdi-child-auxiliary`).
