## 8. ACA premium tax credit (pre-65 retirees)

- Enhanced (ARPA/IRA) credits **expired Dec 31, 2025**. As of 2026: credits available only up to **400% FPL — a hard cliff** ($1 over forfeits the entire credit), with pre-ARPA applicable-percentage scale below it.
- The exact ledger uses **current-year ACA household MAGI**: signed federal AGI before its return-level zero floor + nontaxable Social Security + characterized tax-exempt interest + an explicit foreign earned-income/housing exclusion addback + MAGI of dependents the contract marks required to file, with the household total then floored at zero. Tax-family size is separate from Marketplace-covered members and months. The foreign exclusion remains non-taxable but participates in Social Security provisional income.
- The annual result distinguishes gross enrollment premium, applicable second-lowest-cost Silver plan (SLCSP) benchmark, modeled allowable PTC, and economic net premium. The SLCSP drives the preliminary credit and enrollment premium caps it.
- Premium, tax, withdrawals, and ACA MAGI are solved together on the current-year ledger. Ordinary Marketplace premiums are excluded from HSA-qualified medical-expense capacity. A same-year conversion, gain, or induced taxable withdrawal can reduce or eliminate the modeled credit. The solver is deterministic and hard-bounded; a conflicting subsidized/gross root, unsupported input, or unstable fixed point funds gross premium and emits non-actionable evidence.
- The credit reads its own coverage year's figures (`params/acaCoverageYears.ts`): the Applicable Percentage Table of that year's revenue procedure and the HHS poverty guidelines in effect when that year's open enrollment began, which 26 U.S.C. 36B(d)(3)(B) and 26 CFR 1.36B-1(h) make the guidelines HHS published in January of the year before. 2026 coverage uses Rev. Proc. 2025-25 and the HHS 2025 guidelines (15,650 + 5,500 per added person); 2027 coverage uses Rev. Proc. 2026-26 (2.15% below 133%, then 3.23 to 4.30, 4.30 to 6.78, 6.78 to 8.66, 8.66 to 10.22, and 10.22 from 300% to 400%) and the HHS 2026 guidelines (15,960 + 5,680; Alaska 19,950 + 7,100; Hawaii 18,360 + 6,530). Published guidelines are used as published, never inflated. The table is read as Form 8962 reads it: at the household's poverty-line percentage with the fraction dropped (Worksheet 2, line 4), and the applicable percentage there is rounded to the nearest one-hundredth of one percent (26 CFR 1.36B-3(g)(1)); the 100% and 400% tests use the exact figure. The form's whole-dollar rounding of the annual and monthly contribution (lines 8a and 8b) is not applied, a stated limit worth at most about $6 of credit a year. Each schedule has a real step at exactly 133% FPL, and the poverty tables are separate for the 48 contiguous states/DC, Alaska, and Hawaii. The year contract names one `fplRegion`; the rule in 26 CFR 1.36B-1(h) that takes the higher guideline when the household moves during the year between states with different guidelines, or when spouses live in separate ones, is not applied (a stated limit on `aca-coverage-year-parameters`). Exactly 400% remains eligible; above 400% is over the cliff. Below 100% is non-actionable and funds the gross premium: there is generally no credit there (Medicaid may apply), and the one exception left for tax years after 2025, Treas. Reg. 1.36B-2(b)(6) (advance credit paid on an enrollment-time estimate of 100% to 400%), is not modeled. The lawfully-present exception of 26 U.S.C. 36B(c)(1)(B) was repealed for those years by Pub. L. 119-21 §71302(a).
- A credit-enabled marketplace year requires the additive `expenses.healthcare.acaYears` contract: one living primary, filing-status-consistent spouse topology, unique tax-family and covered-member identities, explicit dependent filing status, enrollment and SLCSP premiums by month, FPL region, characterized addbacks, and no-special-case assertions. Benchmark-only months are not coverage. A legacy credit-enabled year without the contract keeps the gross premium and reports `missing-year-contract`; duplicate contracts preserve the largest known monthly gross premium but are non-actionable.
- Each contract's `premiumBasis` says where its premiums come from (decisions D-EXAMPLE-SOURCE-SWITCH and D-ACA-CONTRACT-PATHS, 2026-09-28; record `aca-contract-premium-basis`). `stated`, the default when absent, is the coverage year's actual figures: used as written on every run and held in nominal dollars on every Monte Carlo path, because a quoted premium does not change with a simulated inflation rate. `premiumField` stores only the year, the assertions and the two characterized amounts; for each run and year the engine fills the FPL region from the state lived in, the tax family from the people alive (the first primary, the next spouse), the covered members alive and under Medicare age, and each covered month's enrollment premium and SLCSP benchmark as `pre65MonthlyPremiumPerPerson` grown by that run's healthcare inflation. A year whose premiums are an estimate belongs in `premiumField`. The projection never reads `exampleSourceId`.
- Coverage ends at death. 45 CFR 155.430(d)(7): "In the case of a termination due to death, the last day of enrollment in a QHP through the Exchange is the date of death." For the credit, 26 U.S.C. 36B(c)(2)(A) counts a month only when the member is enrolled on its first day, so no month after the month of death is a coverage month. A covered member who has died on a run is charged nothing under either basis from 1 January of the year after the death, the ledger's annual convention (a person is alive through the year of the death age for income, benefits, filing status and spending as well), so the rest of the death year is charged: a stated limit. Measured by the independent review of this change (finding L2) on all-401k-no-bridge with its contracts written as stated figures and stochastic longevity, on the 2022 SSA period life table (before decision D-LIFE-TABLE-2023 moved the longevity draw to the 2023 table): 97 of 300 paths (32 percent) have a covered member die in a contract year; at a uniform month of death the year convention overcharges about 5.5 months of that member's premium, about $12,000 on a path that has one ($3,884 averaged over all paths); and it lowers the success rate by about 0.15 points (at 4,000 paths: 31.20 percent as shipped, 31.35 percent charging 6 months of the death year, 31.43 percent charging none), at most 0.23 points. A `stated` tax family that still names that member no longer describes the return, and the year is unpriced with `tax-family-member-unknown`. A `premiumField` family is re-derived from the people alive (the first primary, the next spouse), so the survivor's credit is priced, except in two cases the engine refuses with `tax-family-structure-unsupported`: a qualifying-surviving-spouse year (the structure check wants a dependent, which the derived family never has) and two people filing single (the derived family makes the second a spouse).
- Only coverage years with published ACA figures can be actionable; a later year (2028 onward today) reports `tax-year-parameters-unsupported`, exposes no inflation-scaled FPL as actionable evidence, and funds gross premium. A coverage year whose ACA figures are published while its income-tax figures are not (2027, until the fall's income-tax figures are added) is priced and actionable and says so with the informational code `income-tax-parameters-projected`: the credit formula reads no income-tax figure, but the household's MAGI does, through bracket-sized conversions and the tax fixed point. The `acaCliff` conversion ceiling reads the same coverage-year guidelines. Adaptive spending policies are also blocked with `guardrail-interaction-unsupported` until their spending choice and ACA reconciliation share a supported fixed point.
- Annual Marketplace enrollment must also stop before each covered member's engine-modeled Medicare months. An age-65 transition-month or post-65 overlap reports `medicare-overlap-unsupported`, retains the known gross Marketplace premium alongside Medicare costs, and makes the ACA year non-actionable rather than silently dropping either charge.
- The standard planner UI does not yet author `acaYears`; its legacy premium field and credit toggle remain available. Until a contract editor ships, plans authored only through that UI and enabling the credit intentionally fail closed to gross premium. The library examples carry `premiumField` contracts, and a plan saved from one keeps them: editing the premium re-prices the credit. A premium edit, a change of state, a move, a date of birth or a planning age removes only `stated` contracts; a partner added or removed, or a new filing status, removes both kinds, because a contract's stored assertions are facts about who is on the return (review finding M2). Every removal is recorded on the plan (`healthcare.acaYearsRemoved`) with the edit and the years, and the planner names that edit when it says why a year's credit is not counted. Plans saved from an example before plan schema v6 have the contracts the recipe wrote (its shape, and the premium field grown at one rate, to half a cent) rewritten to `premiumField` on load, with a notice. A contract from the recipe that no longer matched the plan's premium (the premium, the inflation or the household changed after saving) was already left out by the v5 engine, so the migration removes it, records it (`exampleNoLongerMatched`) and says so, and the figures do not change; a contract the v5 engine priced as written, or one not in the recipe's shape, stays `stated` (PR #761 review 2), and one not in the recipe's shape that the v5 engine refused is now priced as entered, which the load announces because its figures change. The contracts a stored scenario writes are sorted by the same rule in the plan the scenario makes. `stated` contracts are currently available through schema/API/import paths.
- This is a **planning-year allowable-credit/economic-premium model**, not actual advance premium tax credit (APTC) cash timing and not a filing-grade Form 8962 refund, repayment, or balance-due reconciliation. Form 8814, special allocations, MFS exceptions, self-employed health-insurance-deduction interactions, and other material unsupported facts fail closed. From 2027 a tax-family member who is lawfully present but not an eligible alien (Pub. L. 119-21 §71301, amending 36B(e)) is not modeled either: the contract's `coverageEligibility: 'supported'` asserts there is none, and `'unsupported'` fails the year closed.
